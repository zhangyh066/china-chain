# Seeing an industrial chain clearly

**Most people look at companies sideways** — a row of tickers, a column of price moves, a pile of ratios, all disconnected.

We look **vertically**: we put a company back inside its chain. And not just to see the structure — to see how it is moving.

## What we are not doing

Wind and Qichacha have perfected the "company dossier": registration, financials, news, one company at a time. That is the limit of looking sideways, and we are not doing it again.

**Our unit of analysis is not the company — it is the chain link**: where a company sits on its chain. Others answer "how is this company doing"; we answer "where does it stand, which way is its link moving, and what is about to happen upstream and downstream." Not better data — a different way of seeing.

## Three layers

### Layer 1: Structure — position before comparison

A flat list of four thousand companies is just a column of tickers. Put each one back on its links and you can read who shares a chain with whom. **Position first, comparison second**: without position, comparison is only ever a size ranking.

### Layer 2: Trend — direction before snapshot

A number is a snapshot; a trend is a direction. We compute an equal-weighted one-year index for the whole market, for all 24 sectors, and for each of the 199 chain links: not how big a link is, but which way it has been heading.

A company's price line no longer stands alone either — company, sector, and link are drawn on the same chart, so you can see at a glance whether it is beating its own link or being carried by it. And the link-temperature board on the home page answers a blunter question: **this week, along which links is money actually moving?**

### Layer 3: Depth — drill one chain through first

The end of breadth is just another way of listing. So we picked lithium batteries and drilled down: a hand-curated chain of nine stages from lithium mining to recycling, forty-seven core companies, with the lithium carbonate futures price laid alongside — when the price jumps at the very top of the chain, you can watch which links move first and which follow.

Why lithium: the chain is long enough, the links are clear-cut, China holds a globally dominant position on it, and lithium carbonate's two-year rollercoaster is the best possible sample. Once this "chain + price + trend" playbook is proven, it gets copied to photovoltaics and semiconductors.

## What this atlas covers now

- **4,071** A-share listed companies (screened down from 5,918 — only those with a real chain position), across **24** SW level-1 sectors
- **199** chain links and **18,404** company-to-link memberships; every link carries its own one-year equal-weighted index
- **26,625** upstream/downstream edges covering 4,057 companies; **151** of them also carry a hand-curated value-chain map
- The lithium board: 9 stages, 47 core companies, and the lithium carbonate front-month futures price
- Quotes, financials, and all indices **refresh automatically every trading day**, with the data date shown on the page

## The data caveat, stated plainly

- Company names, tickers, SW sectors, link membership — **real, public**
- Quotes, financials, company profiles, daily bars and trend indices — **real public data** (East Money · THS · CNINFO · akshare), updated every trading day
- Upstream/downstream edges — **inferred from real industry structure, illustrative and not verified company by company**. Sharing a chain is not a supplier relationship
- The lithium carbonate price is the **front-month futures contract** (GFEX), not spot

## Next: the lag of transmission

The same news means different things at different positions on the chain: a lithium price rise is good news for miners, a cost for material makers, and a quote revision next quarter for cell makers. **Transmission has direction, and it has lag** — the further downstream, the later the news arrives, and the shorter the window to react.

We want to measure that lag and draw it: if lithium carbonate rises 10%, how many days until cathode materials move, and how long after that do cells? Then "reading the chain" stops being about structure and becomes **judging the order before the transmission arrives**.
